Professional Reflection

What I learned in this module

Building this portfolio pushed me past writing code that only I would ever read. I had to think about structure (consistent navigation across pages), accessibility (focus states, alt text), and SEO (meaningful titles and headings) — the parts of a project that matter once someone other than the developer is looking at it.

My strongest IT skill right now

Debugging and problem-solving. With around five years of coding across Delphi, Kotlin, and React Native behind me, I've spent a lot of time tracing errors back to their actual cause rather than just patching symptoms — that habit carried straight over into building this portfolio, where I had to work through layout and navigation issues across five linked pages.

The skill I most want to improve

C#. Second year brings C# and Visual Studio into the picture, and it's the one language on the horizon I haven't touched yet, so it's the clearest gap between where I am now and where the course is heading next.

How I'll develop that skill

I plan to start on small C# console exercises before second year begins rather than meeting the language for the first time in a graded module, and to lean on the same debugging approach that's worked for me in Kotlin and TypeScript — get something running first, then understand why it broke when it inevitably does.

How this portfolio supports my future studies and career

Having one place that links my GitHub, LinkedIn, and real projects means I'm not starting from zero every time I apply for an internship — I can point to this instead of re-explaining my background each time, and I can keep adding to it as I complete more modules.
